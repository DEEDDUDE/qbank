### PH-Q-001
status: verified
answer: b
basis: Ch. 9 — Levels of Prevention {#ch09-4}
evidence: Secondary prevention's interventions are "early diagnosis (screening
  tests, case-finding programs) and adequate treatment." A mammogram is a
  routine screening test aimed at catching an existing cancer in its earliest
  stage — exactly this category, not preventing the cancer from occurring at
  all.

### PH-Q-011
status: conflict
answer: d
claimed: a
basis: Ch. 9 — Levels of Prevention {#ch09-5}
evidence: Rehabilitation (a tertiary-prevention mechanism) is defined as "the
  combined and coordinated use of medical, social, educational, and
  vocational measures for training and retraining the individual to the
  highest possible level of functional ability," including "Social
  rehabilitation (restoration of family and social relationships)." A school
  for children who are already blind is exactly this — adapting education and
  social participation around an existing disability, not preventing a risk
  factor from ever emerging in the population (primordial, the claimed
  answer), which has nothing to do with accommodating an already-present
  impairment.
note: The circulating attempt marks Primordial. The source supports Tertiary.

### PH-Q-012
status: conflict
answer: b
claimed: d
basis: Ch. 9 — Levels of Prevention {#ch09-4}
evidence: Secondary prevention's own definition, word for word: "action which
  halts the progress of a disease at its incipient stage and prevents
  complications." Its objectives list repeats this exactly: "prevent
  complications and sequelae." Type 2 diabetes is an already-diagnosed
  disease here, and the action described is stopping its complications before
  they occur — precisely secondary prevention's job, not primary prevention
  (which by definition acts *before* disease onset).
note: The circulating attempt marks Primary — a genuine error; primary
  prevention cannot apply to a disease that already exists. The source
  supports Secondary.
