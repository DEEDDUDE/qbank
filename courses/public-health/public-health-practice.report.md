## Conflicts — the circulating file is wrong on these 16 (read these first)

The most valuable output of this pass. In every case below, `source.md` supports a different answer than the one bold-marked/claimed in the raw exam material.

- **PH-P-056** — "Health plays a major role in?" claimed **d** ("All of the them"), which absurdly includes "disease development" as a role health plays. Ch.3's "healthy population is also a driver of growth" supports **e/f** (economic development ± poverty reduction) instead.
- **PH-P-063** — "Universal package...helps sustain financing" claimed **False**. Ch.16's pooling/cross-subsidization logic ("wealthy & healthy subsidize poor & sick") directly supports **True**.
- **PH-P-075** — "Multi-Causal Theory...demands change in industrial production" claimed **True**. That description is Ch.2's **Environmental Theory**, not the Multi-Causal Theory — should be **False**.
- **PH-P-095** — "Goal of PH is to guarantee all members achieve optimal health" claimed **True**, inconsistent with the source's own "maximum benefit" framing (Ch.1) and inconsistent with this same file's own PH-P-119/121, which both reject "guarantee" language for PH's goal. Should be **False**.
- **PH-P-190** — "Availability means cost should be within the means..." claimed **True**. That is verbatim Ch.12's definition of **Affordability**, not Availability. Should be **False**.
- **PH-P-204 / PH-P-300** — radiation-treatment/brain-tumor scenario claimed **"Retrospective (case-control)"**. This is structurally identical to Ch.6's own worked example (Chernobyl thyroid cancer) of a **Historical Prospective (cohort) design** — starts from exposure, looks forward to outcome, even though the data collection itself happens in retrospect.
- **PH-P-258** — "Modern epidemiology started with?" claimed **c** (Doll and Hill). Ch.2 explicitly credits **John Snow** (1854, Broad Street Pump) with founding modern epidemiology.
- **PH-P-265** — "Experimental study designs are an example of analytical epidemiology" claimed **True**. This source's own decision tree places Analytical Study strictly as a subtype of *Observational* studies, with Experimental as a separate branch — **False** per this source's own taxonomy (though other textbooks classify it differently).
- **PH-P-272 / PH-P-273** — "odds of being a case among the exposed/unexposed" claimed **64/242** and **166/443** (cases ÷ group total — the *risk* formula). Ch.7's own odds definition and worked CHD example compute odds as cases ÷ non-cases, giving **64/178** and **166/277**.
- **PH-P-277** — "incidence in unexposed" claimed **320/990**. Ch.7's own incidence formula (cases ÷ group total) and the paired RR question's (PH-P-278) own formula both use **320/1310** — the source's own worked answer is internally inconsistent, and 1310 is correct.
- **PH-P-281** — "diet/exercise to prevent further heart attacks/strokes" claimed **Secondary**; Ch.9's Fig.4.1 and the exercise's own cited external reference (AFMC Primer) both match this to **Tertiary** (preventing relapse in an already-diseased patient) more strongly, though the source's Secondary "prevent complications and sequelae" language gives the claim some support too.
- **PH-P-282** — "getting a young person to quit smoking" claimed **Secondary**. The exercise's own cited source (AFMC Primer) lists smoking cessation explicitly as **Primary** prevention.
- **PH-P-286** — "municipality bans hazardous products (asbestos)" claimed **Primary**. Ch.9's "measures typically promoted through laws and national policy" and the AFMC Primer's own near-identical example ("outlawing alcohol") both point to **Primordial**.
- **PH-P-288** — "maintenance of normal body weight through nutrition/exercise" claimed **Primordial**; Ch.9's "Lifestyle and behavioural changes" under Primary contradicts this (and is inconsistent with the near-identical PH-P-290, claimed/verified Primary) — should be **Primary**.
