**Conflicts (2)** — the circulating claims are wrong on both; source wins:

- **PHARM-Q-038** — claimed True ("all penicillins, natural or synthetic, are
  ineffective against *S. aureus*"); the source's own penicillinase-resistant
  ("antistaphylococcal") class — methicillin, oxacillin — exists specifically to
  treat non-MRSA *S. aureus* → False.
- **PHARM-Q-043** — claimed "both penicillin V and amoxicillin" are pure natural
  antimicrobials; the source's natural-penicillin list is Penicillin G and
  Penicillin V only, amoxicillin is filed under semisynthetic aminopenicillins →
  Penicillin V alone.

**Not in source (1)** — **PHARM-Q-039**: asks which cephalosporin is
MRSA-active; the source names exactly one MRSA-active cephalosporin
(Ceftaroline), and it isn't among this question's four options (Cefepime,
Ceftriaxone, Cefixime, Ceftolozane). The claim (Ceftolozane) is contradicted,
but no confident answer can be picked from the four offered.

**External (7)** — settled facts the slides don't happen to state as such:
PHARM-Q-007 (oily solutions need IM, not IV), PHARM-Q-010 (enteric coating's

PHARM-Q-029 (isoniazid — narrow-spectrum concept is in-source, the drug itself
isn't), PHARM-Q-032 (selective toxicity — a resolved dispute, see below),
PHARM-Q-045 (chemical antagonism, a term this source's antagonist
classification doesn't include).

**Disputes resolved (3)** — every disputed question from Job A had a real
source-backed answer, so none stayed disputed in the output:

- **PHARM-Q-032** — two attempts disagreed (Selective toxicity vs. Therapeutic
  level); the term itself isn't in this source, but the standard textbook
  definition and the other claim's off-topic meaning both point to Selective
  toxicity → external, resolved.
- **PHARM-Q-034** — 2 of 3 attempts claimed Tazobactam, 1 claimed "None of
  them"; the source's own classification tree files tazobactam under
  β-lactamase inhibitors, separate from the β-lactam-antibiotic branch →
  Tazobactam confirmed.
- **PHARM-Q-035** — attempts disagreed between "could be given orally" and
  "effective against MRSA"; the source confirms broad-spectrum penicillins are
  orally absorbed and explicitly says they don't overcome β-lactamase/MRSA
  resistance → "could be given orally" confirmed.
