## Conflicts — the circulating file is wrong on these 15

- **MGEN-M-077** — claimed "eukaryotes have nuclear ribosomes" (false — source
  places them in the cytoplasm/RER). The circled answer was already
  self-retracted (crossed out) without a replacement; neither mark on the page
  reached the source-supported answer, "initiation usually begins at an AUG
  codon."
- **MGEN-M-083** — claimed a missense mutation is most likely to cause loss of
  gene function. A frameshift mutation (disrupting the entire downstream
  reading frame) is the standard answer.
- **MGEN-M-084** — claimed tube recipe "a" (high dNTP/low ddATP/DNA pol) is
  the Sanger *exception*, but that recipe is exactly what source describes as
  correct. Recipe "c" (RNA polymerase instead of DNA polymerase) is the actual
  outlier.
- **MGEN-M-087** — claimed SRP's ER-targeting role is the "NOT a property"
  answer, but source directly confirms SRP does target nascent polypeptides to
  the RER. Signal peptidase activity — a separate ER enzyme SRP itself doesn't
  have — is the real exception.
- **MGEN-M-089** — claimed telomerase uses a DNA template with an RNA primer;
  reversed. Telomerase (an RT) uses its RNA as template and extends the
  chromosome's own 3' DNA end as primer.
- **MGEN-M-095** — claimed "not involved in mRNA processing" = splicing +
  transport. Splicing IS a processing step per source; only transport is
  genuinely excluded, and no listed option captures that cleanly.
- **MGEN-M-103** — claimed "remove RNA primers" is something DNA polymerases
  *don't* do — but pol I does exactly this (nick translation). "Initiate
  replication" is the real exception (polymerases always need a primer).
- **MGEN-M-113** — claimed peptidyl transferase activity resides in "a charged
  tRNA molecule." Source places it on the ribosome (large-subunit rRNA, a
  ribozyme).
- **MGEN-M-117** — both marks on the page are wrong: neither "stem-loop in the
  DNA template" (circled) nor "Rho has topoisomerase activity" (margin key)
  matches source. The stem-loop forms in the RNA transcript, and Rho is a
  helicase, not a topoisomerase.
- **MGEN-M-126** — claimed polysomes forming during ongoing transcription is a
  eukaryotic feature. Source attributes coupled transcription-translation to
  *prokaryotes*; eukaryotic transcription and translation are physically
  separated.
- **MGEN-M-133** — claimed translocation "requires... energy from ATP." Source
  frames elongation as GTPase-driven throughout (EF-Tu/EF-G), not ATP-driven.
- **MGEN-M-148** — word-bank claim "A" (Primase) doesn't fit the question's own
  "in Eukaryotes" qualifier; source's exonuclease-deficient, short-primer
  eukaryotic enzyme is specifically DNA polymerase alpha ("E").
- **MGEN-M-149** — word-bank claim "E" (DNA polymerase alpha) doesn't fit
  O6-methylguanine repair at all; source's methyltransferase mechanism matches
  "I" (Methyltransferase) — likely a letter transcription slip in the original
  claim.
- **MGEN-M-178** — claimed telomerase adds repeats to "3' and 5' ends." Its own
  direct catalytic action extends only the 3' end; the complementary strand is
  filled in afterward by ordinary replication machinery, not telomerase.
- **MGEN-M-219** — claimed positive supercoiling ahead of an advancing
  replication fork is false. Source states this exact overwinding-ahead
  mechanism explicitly for the analogous transcription case — the general
  topology principle applies to replication too.

MGEN-M-068/151/152/186/233 all turn on the same underlying fact: this
course's own word-bank items (MGEN-M-151, MGEN-M-152) establish that **RecA**
forms the ssDNA filament and **RecBCD** does the Chi-sequence-recognizing,
asymmetric DNA degradation — not the reverse. Two separate true/false items
(MGEN-M-186, MGEN-M-233) that attribute RecBCD's behavior to "RecA" are false
for this reason, independent of any other issue with those items.
